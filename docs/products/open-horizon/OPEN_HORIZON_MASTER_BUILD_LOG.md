## 00 | Agent activation and operating contract

**Project:** Open Horizon: Cinematic Commercial Experience System (working title)  
**Version:** 1.0 research-to-build specification, 25 October 2026  
**Status:** Proposed / unbuilt. No implementation, live integration, user studies or conversion uplift is claimed.  
**Intent:** Give a delivery agent a sufficiently detailed, executable specification for discovery, divergent ideation, prototyping, validation and eventual delivery of a visual-first AI marketing agency website and repeatable product platform.

### Start command (copy into your agent)
> ACTIVATE OPEN HORIZON v1.0. Treat this document as the working product requirements and discovery contract. Begin with repository/environment reconnaissance and the independent research tracks. Produce traceable artifacts and execute unblocked low-risk work autonomously. Run parallel research when possible. Never confuse research, mockups or demonstrations with tested or deployed capabilities. Follow approval boundaries and make progress visible through evidence receipts. If repository access is absent, create a portable project skeleton and backlog rather than fabricating implementation status. Do not treat my examples as limits on the research space.

### Mandate
Create a marketing agency experience that is simultaneously: visually cinematic, genuinely interactive, commercially useful, easy to comprehend, search-discoverable, accessible, fast, measurable, and capable of learning from controlled experiments. Treat **Higgsfield** and **Artlist** as preferred creative-production sources, but investigate additional tools without loyalty or vendor lock-in. The agent must explore far outside the category of marketing websites.

### Guardrails
- Discovery before irreversible build; different hypotheses before aesthetic convergence.
- Reuse mature libraries over custom engines where sensible. A visually impressive clip is **not** a manipulable 3D object.
- No fabricated analytics, research sources, ROI, case studies, pricing, client reviews, authorisations or deployment results.
- Do not purchase credits, subscribe to services, publish public-facing work, access customer data, or deploy production without explicit authority.
- Respect third-party licensing; confirm export/commercial use, music synchronisation, model rights, attribution and model/data provenance per asset.
- Use consent-aware first-party instrumentation, data minimisation, retention limits and security/privacy review.
- Every claim of 'complete' requires a linked, replayable verification receipt.
- Maintain a running decision log, hypothesis ledger, cost ledger, risks, failed experiments and next-action queue.

### Definition of success
Not merely a striking hero animation: real visitors can identify what the agency offers, find an appropriate next step, operate a meaningful demo, and convert through an attributable path on desktop and mobile. The platform can test alternative experiences without losing stability, SEO semantics, privacy, or accessibility.

## 01 | Problem framing and pivotal video analysis

### Initial hypothesis
Low-text, visual-first marketing agency experience using cinematic footage, CAD-like exploded views, spatial data, 3D schematics, product demonstrations, and agent-directed creative production.

### Insight from supplied 30-minute video transcript
The speaker uses **digital café / digital home** as an analogy and argues that a site should not be a passive brochure. His concepts are: (1) owned digital presence / digital sovereignty; (2) multiple **collection points** such as products, free resources, events and services; (3) diverse **value ladder** for the same audience; (4) the central site as a home connecting acquisition channels and focused pages; (5) iterative launches that learn from failures; (6) five coordinated layers: offer, copy, content, landing page, funnel; (7) AI search increasingly affecting discovery and due diligence.

**Source provenance:** user-provided transcript of https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xwF08pXuTJQ, chapters roughly 0:00–30:10. Interpret these as presenter assertions and commercial hypotheses, NOT independently validated universal facts. Numeric claims about daily site creation, inactive sites, conversion rates, email revenue and AI referral conversion require original source/method verification. Do not assume 'more offers = more income' or 'more mentions = automatic AI recommendation'.

### Reframed product
**One visual world, many purposeful pathways:** cinematic presentation -> service comprehension -> opt-in utility/demo -> intent-qualified offer -> focused transaction/booking -> consent-aware nurturing -> measurement -> controlled iteration.

**Key distinction:** a scene 'interaction' is not automatically a 'collection point'. A collection point is an attributable exchange of value or advancement of a business relationship: e.g. receiving a diagnosis, requesting an estimate, purchasing a package or booking a consultation.

### Research tension to test
Does visual novelty increase trust and useful discovery, or distract from immediate intent? Run side-by-side comparisons with a well-made conventional interface. Every cinematic element must justify its effect on user understanding, motivation, accessibility and results.

## 02 | Six dimensions and anti-bubble research engine

Run six parallel research programmes, each with disconfirming research:

| Dimension | Investigate | Crossovers / proof questions |
|---|---|---|
| D1 Physical engineering | CAD, exploded assemblies, digital twins, parametrisation, technical illustrations, robotics, architecture | Can a visitor manipulate an agency 'machine' to understand products? |
| D2 Human perception | Gestalt, attentional guidance, memory, cognitive load, motion sensitivity, information scent, accessible multimodality | Can a new visitor find their task within seconds without reading a wall of text? |
| D3 Cinema | cinematography, editing, camera grammar, practical VFX, interactive documentary, set design | Can generated motion guide rather than hijack attention? |
| D4 Games and simulation | real-time engines, physics, progressive disclosure, game onboarding, virtual exhibitions | Can playful exploration become an intuitive practical tool? |
| D5 Living intelligence | agent orchestration, conversational navigation, adaptive UI, live data visualisation, recommendation systems | Can AI create useful, bounded assistance rather than fake personalisation? |
| D6 Commercial ecosystem intelligence | offers, collection points, value ladders, lead qualification, first-party measurement, experimentation, lifecycle | What can a visitor actually obtain or buy today and what happens next? |

### Required divergence process
1. For each dimension gather >=8 independently sourced precedents from at least 3 unrelated fields; >=48 total source leads. Mix vendor docs, real implementations, reputable UX scholarship, open-source and critical reviews.
2. Extract mechanisms, NOT aesthetics: why does an example work, what transferable principle is there, what would invalidate it?
3. Generate >=40 **structurally distinct** product/experience concepts, including >=15 cross-discipline hybrids. No cosmetic 'same layout but blue' counting.
4. Run a hostile critic pass on every idea: necessity, user benefit, manufacturability, accessibility, cost, discoverability, commercial hypothesis, privacy, failure modes.
5. Recombine at least 12 low-similarity ideas, including wildcards from an outside discipline not listed above.
6. Advance 8 shortlisted experiments and 3 materially different working prototypes, including a no-3D baseline.
7. Prioritise by evidence and cheap falsification. Do not declare a winner based on elegance alone.

### Anti-bubble tactics
- Separate **first-principles**, **analogical**, **counterfactual**, **extreme constraint** and **opposite thesis** groups. They must submit independent ideas before seeing each other's summaries.
- Forced uncomfortable questions: 'What if 3D is the wrong medium?', 'What would a 90-year-old or keyboard-only user do?', 'How would a museum, airport control room, medical scanner or gaming studio solve it?', 'What if visitors arrive already ready to buy?'
- Novelty distance check: identify changed interaction, buyer outcome, commercial mechanism, technical architecture and evidence. Reject clones dressed in alternative visuals.
- A source-quality rubric: primary documentation > reproduced working demo > independent testing > opinion > marketing claim. Record publication date and limitations.

## 04 | Cross-disciplinary recombinations and 12 research provocations

1. Aircraft exploded diagram × AI agency: components represent the exact work required to deliver a service; test comprehension versus cards.
2. Radiology layered scan × website auditing: expose measured problems progressively; do not infer unmeasurable business outcomes from visuals.
3. Museum docent × voice agent: opt-in guided tour with captions and stop controls; test comfort and privacy.
4. Flight simulator × campaign planning: tweak assumptions, see cost and uncertainty; label all outputs hypothetical.
5. Scientific control room × real campaign dashboard: show evidence hierarchy, not gratuitous fake live animations.
6. Game quest onboarding × service selection: teach via deliberate small acts, never artificially delay a visitor who wants pricing.
7. Film title choreography × navigation: context-specific transitions that never block links or back-button.
8. Interactive documentary × case studies: explore inputs, method, receipts and outcomes rather than a promotional showreel.
9. CAD product configurator × website builder: assembling site modules also creates a real structured requirements record.
10. Origami mathematics × progressive disclosure: folding interfaces expose detail on demand, with semantic HTML alternative.
11. Biological systems × content network: show interconnected discovery surfaces without claiming guaranteed search ranking.
12. Marketplace storefront × value ladder: multiple relevant collection points, but keep customer intent and clear hierarchy first.

Further agent challenge: independently invent >=12 additional pairings from truly non-adjacent disciplines, then test novelty against published implementations. Label originality claims carefully.

## 05 | Product architecture, journeys and collection points

### Core experience architecture
1. **Arrival:** lightweight HTML content and striking but non-blocking poster/frame; visitors understand positioning immediately.
2. **Orient:** choose outcome: Discover, Diagnose, Create, Explore Results, or Talk. Expose normal nav as fallback.
3. **Demonstrate:** launch optional 3D/cinematic module tied to one customer job.
4. **Deliver value:** usable mini-tool, demo output or downloadable report with honest evidence.
5. **Collection point:** one clear next step per product page: buy / book / submit requirements / opt in.
6. **Follow-up:** consent-aware, preference-based value delivery, with unsubscribe and data deletion mechanisms.
7. **Review:** measure qualified outcomes and compare tested experience variants.

### Starter collection-point catalogue
| Product | Free value | Paid entry | Ongoing step | Main event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Website design | Interactive site configurator | Scoped design package | Managed maintenance | qualified_quote |
| SEO/AEO discovery | Diagnostic overview | In-depth audit | Search optimisation retainer | audit_purchase |
| Creative ads | Limited storyboard preview | Video/asset package | Iterative campaigns | creative_checkout |
| AI answering | Safe simulated dialogue | Setup and configuration | Managed agent service | demo_to_booking |
| CRO | Funnel friction preview | Conversion audit | Experiment programme | CRO_consult |
| Analytics | Sample visual observatory | Tracking setup | Reporting subscription | analytics_signup |
| Brand identity | Brand directions moodboard | Identity sprint | Asset library management | brand_brief |
| Training/resource | Free practical micro-guide | Workshop/course | Community/updates | resource_to_sale |

Never introduce paid offers solely to increase menu count. Ensure every offer serves a real customer segment, has a clear fulfilment process and achievable unit economics.

### Offer-page pattern
Single primary promise, demonstrated proof, key requirements, price or transparent quotation logic, risks/exclusions, clear CTA, and accessible FAQ. Search-visible structured content must coexist with the visual surface: meaningful headings, readable copy, alt text, transcripts, server-rendered metadata and truthful schema.

### Example primary visitor journeys
- **Ready buyer:** landing → immediately see relevant offer/pricing → buy/book (no forced cinematic detour).
- **Curious evaluator:** hero → manipulate 3D demo → comparison → select a product → request scoped estimate.
- **AI-search referral:** precise answer page → cited evidence → hands-on demonstration → product offer.
- **Returning customer:** bookmarked service or secure dashboard → result/next action → repeat purchase or support.
- **Mobile/reduced-motion visitor:** static but fully functional experience with equivalent access and conversions.

## 06 | Technical architecture and production pipeline

### Recommended initial stack (decision pending prototype)
- **Application:** TypeScript + React with a server-rendering capable framework (e.g. Next.js). Semantic HTML, accessible forms and a reliable ordinary navigation system are core; visuals are progressive enhancements.
- **3D runtime:** React Three Fiber + Three.js for complex stateful live 3D; evaluate Spline embeds for simple authored interactions. Avoid embedding heavyweight full scenes before intent is clear.
- **Asset preparation:** Blender for mesh cleanup, re-topology, LODs, UV/material optimisation, export QA; compressed GLB/glTF, KTX2 textures and Draco or Meshopt where supported.
- **Creative production:** Higgsfield for storyboard/short cinematic footage/advertising concepts; Artlist for media and textured 3D asset generation; human QC for consistency and licensing. Maintain source files, metadata and alternatives.
- **Video delivery:** compressed adaptive outputs / poster frames; user-triggered or viewport-based playback; captions/transcripts; default mute; no forced audio.
- **Backend:** modular services for product catalogue, configuration, enquiries, booking/order integration, entitlements, content, experiments and analytics. Require authenticated access for client-specific metrics.
- **Data:** first-party event store with transparent consent, retention and export. Never render arbitrary private client data into public scenes.
- **Deploy:** preview environments first; CI/test security and performance; monitored feature flags, rollback and immutable version receipts.

### Design-system guidance
Bright, calm, high-contrast design that is not dependent on dark cyberpunk imagery. Large compositionally precise physical objects, plenty of space, clear physical relationships, cinematic quality without gratuitous motion. Allow user-selected display themes if useful. Legibility trumps spectacle. Real navigation labels should remain visible.

### Core domain model
`Product(id, name, promise, segment, price_mode, fulfilment_type, status)`  
`CollectionPoint(id, product_id, offer_id, exchange_type, primary_cta, page_id)`  
`Experience(id, concept_id, scene_asset_id, fallback_id, enabled, accessibility_status)`  
`Asset(id, provider, model, prompt_hash, license_ref, provenance, export_format, bytes, lod_level)`  
`Experiment(id, hypothesis, eligible_population, allocation, success_metric, guardrails, decision, owner)`  
`Event(id, anonymous_or_consent_id, session_id, experience_id, collection_point_id, name, timestamp, consent_state)`  
`EvidenceReceipt(id, commit_sha, environment, test_suite, logs_path, screenshot_path, reviewed_by, timestamp)`.

### Data pipeline
External discovery roads -> semantic offer pages -> optional interactive scene -> clear collection point -> configured CRM/order system -> customer follow-up -> aggregated insight -> experiment registry -> reviewed change. Preserve source/consent on ingest, access controls and deletion policy throughout.

### Required asset flow
Brief -> prompt/reference -> Higgsfield/Artlist draft -> artistic and factual QC -> mesh/video optimisation -> alternate mobile/static assets -> licence evidence -> staging import -> performance/accessibility smoke test -> release candidate.

### Engineering failure modes
Heavy GPU/WebGL on mobile, poor input mapping, motion sickness, 3D model format mismatch, lost navigation focus, blocked search crawlers, fabricated-looking live dashboards, generative inconsistency, runaway generation costs and fragile vendor APIs. Build fallbacks, deterministic scenes and budget limits first.

## 07 | Experiments, evidence and metrics

### Eight first experiments
| Exp | Hypothesis | Treatment | Control | Primary decision metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | Exploded CAD interface improves service comprehension | Interactive engine | Conventional accessible service cards | Correct service-task match |
| E02 | Living sculpture improves exploration | Transforming 3D hub | Static high-quality illustration | Qualified service discovery |
| E03 | Creative lab builds intent | Functional product demo | Video walkthrough | Qualified enquiry rate |
| E04 | Website X-Ray creates demand | Evidence-based diagnostic | Plain checklist audit | Completed diagnostic → next step |
| E05 | Voice interface aids specific visitors | Opt-in guided voice tour | Text/chat guided tour | Task completion, opt-out, privacy comfort |
| E06 | Multiple collection points improve revenue | Curated relevant offer paths | Single-offer structure | Revenue per eligible visitor with guardrails |
| E07 | Personalised routing reduces friction | Explicit goal selector | Ordinary nav | Time to meaningful next action |
| E08 | Cinematic entry improves trust | Short cinematic reveal | Still hero | Comprehension + performance + qualified conversion |

**Baseline:** Every experience gets a semantic no-WebGL version and a no-cinematic control. Random allocation where suitable, minimum sample planning, primary metric defined before launch, confidence/uncertainty reported. Avoid significance fishing and tiny-sample 'winners'.

### KPI hierarchy
- **North star (business):** qualified revenue per eligible visitor (when enough data exists); prior leading signals include booked qualified consultations and paid orders.
- **UX:** first-use comprehension, task completion, time to offer discovery, navigation recovery, self-reported confidence, accessibility failures.
- **Performance:** mobile p75 LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1 as target Core Web Vitals; WebGL frame budget measured by device tier; graceful fallback.
- **Commercial:** offer view → demo → value capture → qualified lead → booked meeting → paid purchase; cancellation, refund and fulfilment costs.
- **Economics:** generation credits used, creative minutes/assets accepted, cost per qualified lead, gross margin, maintenance cost.
- **Trust:** factual errors, user complaints, consent issues, broken promises, misleading outputs.

### Instrumentation event taxonomy
`page_view`, `offer_view`, `scene_load_start`, `scene_ready`, `scene_fallback`, `scene_interact`, `demo_started`, `demo_completed`, `diagnostic_started`, `diagnostic_completed`, `collection_point_view`, `cta_clicked`, `lead_submitted`, `meeting_booked`, `checkout_started`, `purchase_completed`, `opt_in`, `consent_updated`, `error_observed`, `asset_cost_recorded`.

### Example funnel definition
For a defined traffic cohort and period: visitors -> eligible offer viewers -> demo starts -> completions -> CTAs -> qualified conversions. Report unique denominators at each stage, attribution uncertainty, traffic source, device, consent sample and experiment variant. Never compare metrics with incompatible populations as though equivalent.

### Release gates
**G0 Discovery:** credible primary sources, real divergent concepts, traceable hypotheses.  
**G1 Feasibility:** prototypes use functioning interactions, not screenshots passed off as software.  
**G2 Safety/access:** WCAG 2.2 AA-oriented audit, keyboard and screen reader checks, reduced motion, security/privacy/licensing review.  
**G3 Reliability:** CI tests, device matrix, performance, regression, fallback and rollback verified.  
**G4 Commercial:** offers fulfilled end to end, event attribution, payment/booking permissions and evidence-based claims.  
**G5 Release:** explicit deployment approval, verified production SHA, smoke tests and owner signoff.

## 08 | Parallel agent organisation and governance

### Team structure
- **Research Orchestrator:** sources, novelty distance, cross-track synthesis; cannot declare unsupported 'best'.
- **Research Track A: engineering/3D:** CAD, real-time scene feasibility, WebGL trade-offs.
- **Track B: cognition/UX/accessibility:** validation plan and critical failures.
- **Track C: film/media:** Higgsfield/Artlist capabilities, repeatable pipelines, rights.
- **Track D: gaming/spatial interaction:** interaction design and progressive onboarding.
- **Track E: AI/product systems:** conversational route, observability, security boundaries.
- **Track F: commercial ecology:** collection points, value ladders, acquisition, economics.
- **Critical Judge:** independently attacks claims, conflicts and costs; records red flags.
- **Technical Lead:** prototyping architecture and testing.
- **Experiment Lead:** hypotheses, A/B strategy, analytics definitions, outcome adjudication.
- **Delivery Manager:** milestones, cross-team dependencies, approvals, receipts and handover.

### Operating loop
DISCOVER → DIVERGE → SYNTHESISE → CHALLENGE → PROTOTYPE → TEST → DECIDE → IMPLEMENT → VERIFY → LEARN.

### Parallelisation rules
Different research tracks can work concurrently; visual prototypes can start after a shared lean design brief. Never parallel-edit the same uncoordinated production files. Maintain central artifacts and ownership. Human approval is required for spending, external data access, public deployments and irreversible changes. Research and local non-destructive prototyping should continue without repetitive permission requests.

### Update/interrupt contract
Emit milestone reports when an evidence receipt is available, not every meaningless action. Report: completed and verified, work in progress, hypothesis invalidated, blockers, cost consumed, next executable tasks and explicit authority needed. If blocked on one branch, continue independent permitted work. Never claim background execution absent a real scheduler.

### Definition of Done (per task)
Named deliverable exists; acceptance test passes; source or output traceable; version/owner recorded; uncertainties documented; screenshot or test evidence attached; relevant backlog state updated; failure and rollback path understood. 'Generated' ≠ 'tested'; 'tested locally' ≠ 'deployed'; 'deployed' ≠ 'commercially validated'.

## 10 | Concrete prototype acceptance criteria

**Mechanical Intelligence (A)**
- Loads a visually coherent optimised asset or predictable fallback.
- At least four actual clickable/keyboard-operable components map to services; each has readable label, concise benefit, proof link and specific CTA.
- A reset camera view and sensible focus management work; page remains usable without WebGL and when reduced motion is enabled.
- At least five participants (formative, not statistical proof) can attempt independent service-identification tasks; record outcomes and friction.

**Living Sculpture (B)**
- Service choice causes a stateful and meaningful change rather than arbitrary visual flourish.
- No autoplay audio, no orientation traps, pause/reduce-motion support, understandable selection state, clear pricing/next-action links.
- Capture qualitative comprehension and preference relative to static baseline.

**Creative Laboratory (C)**
- User can configure and generate a bounded output (initially deterministic mock outputs if live creative accounts not authorised, clearly labelled 'prototype').
- Output explicitly separates sample/preview from deliverable; show estimated cost and any generation limits.
- Lead brief persists only with opt-in consent and appropriate privacy controls; request/booking flow traceable.

**Cross-cutting:** no horizontal overflow at common device widths, keyboard-only completion of purchase/booking journey, semantic SSR offer content, broken integrations reported honestly, analytics events reconciled with completed transactions and error logs.

## 11 | Risk register and decision log

| Risk | Early indicator | Mitigation / rollback |
|---|---|---|
| Spectacle overwhelms message | Poor first-use comprehension | Static direct hero, simplify scene, test against control |
| High 3D cost or device load | Failed Core Web Vitals, GPU crash | Lazy loading, optimisation, tiered assets, static fallback |
