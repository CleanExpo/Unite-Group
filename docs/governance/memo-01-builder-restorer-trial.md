## Verdict

**Pre-announcement. No public footprint.** The claim that national trials are splitting the
builder/restorer model — a contractor must be one or the other, not both — is recorded as a
founder-reported insider signal from the RIA meeting of 2026-09-07 [CARD-13 unverified]. No Australian
regulator, standards body or industry association page carried it on the domains checked
[TRIAL-07 unverified].

That negative is bounded, and the bound matters: ABCB and QBCC were searched but never
directly fetched [TRIAL-07 unverified]. RIA Australasia's own conference and standards pages carry no
matching announcement [TRIAL-06 unverified]. Neither finding proves no trial exists. They prove the
trial is not public where a public trial would normally appear.

## The four nearest public signals

**1. The restoration industry just wrote its own code.** RIA Australasia has published a
Restoration Industry Code of Practice (RICOP), described as a step toward unifying the
sector [TRIAL-01 unverified]. It is member-only, names no builder/restorer split, and shows no
effective date [TRIAL-01 unverified]. An industry writing its own code is what happens shortly before,
or shortly after, someone else writes one for it.

**2. The split already exists commercially.** At least one insurer's claims process treats
builder/repairer and restorer as two distinct panel roles [TRIAL-02 unverified]. That page does not say
whether one firm may hold both [TRIAL-02 unverified]. A commercial separation is the usual precursor to
a licensing separation.

**3. Licensing friction is on the national agenda.** ICA is reported to be asking governments
to use the mutual-recognition review to ease trade-licensing friction driving repair costs
[TRIAL-03 unverified]. That source is trade press, not an ICA page, and contains no builder/restorer
distinction at all [TRIAL-03 unverified]. It shows the licensing door is open, not that anyone has
walked through it.

**4. Competence verification is being argued in public.** Industry commentary is openly
asking whether insurers should require formal accreditation of restoration contractors
[TRIAL-04 unverified]. That is an opinion piece by a restoration-franchise executive, documenting no
trial [TRIAL-04 unverified].

Separately, a national mould-remediation standard is reported as adopted [TRIAL-05,
STD-12], but neither source was fetched directly and neither carries a usable quote
[TRIAL-05, STD-12].

## What this means

The mechanism a split would need already exists in three places: insurer panel taxonomy
[TRIAL-02 unverified], a live licensing-reform channel [TRIAL-03 unverified], and adopted procedure standards
that define restoration work as its own discipline [STD-01, STD-12]. What is missing is any
public instrument that says a contractor must choose.

The founder's signal stays labelled founder-reported [CARD-13 unverified] until a primary source
carries it. It is not downgraded and it is not promoted.
